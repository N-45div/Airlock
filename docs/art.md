# Held Breath

*The generative art of Airlock: what the site draws, and why.*

An airlock is a held breath. Between two pressures it keeps a small, deliberate silence, and nothing
crosses until it has been looked at. *Held Breath* treats that pause as a physical system. Code arrives
as drift, a population of particles carried by an invisible current, and the chamber is not a wall
but a field: a region where the current bends, slows, and decides. Beauty here is never a picture of
safety. It is the visible residue of a decision being made, frame after frame, by forces no one
draws directly.

The current is a curl of layered noise: divergence-free, so the drift never piles up or tears, only
folds. Several octaves are tuned against each other until the flow reads as weather rather than
static, broad laminar sheets far from the chamber and tight eddies near it, where the decision
happens. Every coefficient is the product of painstaking calibration: the octave ratios, the curl
step, the decay of each trail. It should feel like a meticulously crafted instrument, refined
through countless iterations by someone at the top of the craft, never like noise left on.

Most particles are ordinary, and ordinary things equalize: they enter the chamber cool and leave it
faintly lit, released into the far side's quieter flow. A few carry something, a key, and the
chamber knows. Those slow at the seal, fall into its contour and orbit it, brightening as they are
held, until the seal itself is drawn by everything it refused to let through. No particle is
scripted. The ring of held light is emergent: the accumulation of a simple rule (keyed things meet
a tangential force at the boundary) applied by a master-level implementation thousands of times a
second.

The chamber's outline is a quiet quotation. It is a closed Edwards curve, x² + y² = 1 + d·x²·y², the
family of curves whose twisted cousin carries the signatures every Airlock verdict depends on. Its
softness, somewhere between circle and square, is set by d, and d comes from the seed. Most viewers
will see a rounded chamber. Those who sign things for a living will recognize the shape of the
proof.

Every verdict gets its own sigil, seeded by the receipt it can be checked against. The outer ring is
the receipt hash itself, 256 ticks, one per bit, so two sigils match exactly when their receipts do,
and the art is a fingerprint rather than decoration. Inside, streamlines traced through that seed's
current settle into the seal; their color is the verdict, and the number of held lights is the number
of wallets the sandbox caught leaving. The same seed always yields the same sigil: reproducibility is
not a feature of the art, it is the art's whole claim. The algorithm is meant to read as a
master-level implementation, deeply considered and painstakingly tuned, so that something as dry
as a hash becomes the thing you remember a verdict by.
